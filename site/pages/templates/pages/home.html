Sewing patterns that redraw to fit.

OpenSloper places every point with a formula that reads your measurements. Change the waist, and every line, curve and piece follows. Start drafting and printing in your browser with nothing to install, or download the full set of desktop apps.

Open Design Beta Open Publish Beta Download {{ version }} Get started

Free and open source under GPLv3. The browser apps need no sign-up, and your patterns and measurements stay on your computer. The desktop apps run on Linux, macOS and Windows.

In your browser, nothing to install Beta

The Design, Publish and Measurements apps run in the browser on the same engine as the desktop apps, so formulas, names and undo follow the same rules and files open in both. They're a beta: try them, keep copies of your work, and tell us what breaks.

What the beta does

  • Draft on the canvas with points, lines, curves, circles and rectangles that snap as on the desktop, with rulers, labels, notes, callouts and SVG stamps. The Line and Curve tools draft a whole outline, and a point placed the wrong way can be changed to another type.
  • Make pieces with a click, then add notches, seams, materials, allowances and cut counts, and zips, darts, hems, facings and buttons from the Features tab.
  • Work with formulas: variables, measurements and size tables, name completion, and the wand's formula suggestions.
  • See it sewn in the Garment tab, and find what's wrong in the Problems tab and by the faint red halo on the canvas: click a problem to go to it. Faint arrows show what an object is built from, and what is built from it.
  • Edit as you expect: cut, copy and paste, undo and redo, and import variables from another pattern.
  • Print at true size as a tiled PDF with a test square, or export the draft as SVG.
  • Install them and work offline, once they have loaded.
  • Keep measurements in the Measurements app: one person's or a size table of several sizes, with the catalogue, the body figure, a check against a pattern, and Seamly2D and Valentina import and export.
  • Lay out and print in the Publish app: place, turn and pack the pieces of several patterns in their sizes, add notes and an information block, see how much material to buy, and print on home printer pages, one large sheet or the material, or export PDF, SVG or DXF-AAMA.

Your files stay on your computer

The apps open and save patterns, layouts and measurement files on your own computer and never upload them; the site keeps no patterns, measurements or accounts. Work you haven't saved is kept in your browser's storage, on your machine.

Chrome and Edge save straight back to your files, and find a layout's patterns in a folder you choose. Other browsers open files as uploads to the page and save them as downloads.

Found a problem? Report it on GitHub and choose opensloper-web.

Built the way pattern makers work

Draft a block, derive styles from it, and keep everything tied to the measurements, from the first line to the printed pieces. Everything here is in the desktop apps; the browser beta has the drafting and printing described above.

Formulas, not fixed sizes

Points, lines and curves are placed by formulas that read your measurements and variables, such as a quarter of the waist plus ease.

Formula suggestions

Typed a plain number? The wand beside the field suggests formulas that give about the same value, such as @waist/4, and shows each one at the smallest and largest sizes, so a formula that only fits by chance stands out.

Draw freely, snap to relationships

Draw on the canvas the way you would in a drawing program, a whole outline with the Line tool. Each snap records how a point was placed, so it keeps its place when measurements change. Placed a point the wrong way? Change its type and everything built on it stays.

Click inside to make a piece

No tracing outlines point by point. Pieces get seam allowances, notches, grainlines, materials and cut counts.

Seams that check themselves

Pair the edges that sew together and their lengths are checked as the pattern changes. Problems are listed in plain words.

Sewing details in one step

The Features tab adds a zip, a dart, a hem, a facing or a row of buttons and buttonholes: the points, edges, allowances, seams, notches and topstitching, even the facing piece, as ordinary objects that one Undo takes off. Shared sizes such as the hem depth or button count are variables, so changing one updates them all.

Problems you can see

Anything wrong gets a faint red halo on the canvas and a line in the Problems tab; click one to go to it. Point at a point or edge and faint arrows show what it is built from, and what is built from it.

Made for more than fabric

Designed from the start for leather, foam board, card and other materials as well as fabric. Clothing, bags, wallets, hats and belts, with circles, arcs, rounded corners and output for cutting machines. The tutorials include a lined tote bag and a leather card wallet.

True-scale printing

Tiled PDFs with a test square and matching marks, Greek and Cyrillic text, printer calibration, large-format and projector output, and SVG or DXF-AAMA export.

Size tables and test sizes

Grade a pattern across a whole size table and switch sizes in a click. Test size nudges every measurement up or down to see how the draft grows before you trust it.

Curve paths and offset edges

Smooth armholes, necklines and crotch curves through several points, and edges that follow others at a distance for facings, linings and topstitching.

Copies that follow

Rotate, mirror and move copies of points and edges for dart moves and slash-and-spread. The copies follow their sources when the pattern changes.

Repeats for buttons and pleats

Evenly spaced rows of points for buttons, buttonholes, pleats, tucks and belt loops. The count is a formula too, so the row grows with the pattern. Buttonholes are drawn as slots with bar tacks, and cut as slits by machines.

Notes that do the sums

Put sewing instructions and callouts on the pattern, in the font you choose, bold or italic, embedded when you print to PDF. Notes can hold live values, such as the finished waist, or the piece's name, cut count and size, worked out again whenever the pattern changes.

Pieces laid out for printing

Arrange every piece on the pages before you print: drag, turn and flip them, or let Pack pieces nest them by their shapes onto as few pages as it can, a small piece tucked into the curve of a larger one. Mirrored copies stay flipped, and warnings flag pieces that overlap or run off the page.

How much material

Give a material its roll width, or the size of a sheet of leather or board, and every piece is checked to fit it. Publish works out how much of each material the layout needs, cut folded or single layer, and can print it in the information block.

See it sewn

The Garment tab shows your pieces as sewn while you draft: darts closed, pieces cut on the fold opened out, fold lines such as pleats drawn, and each piece shaded in its material. Export it as SVG or PNG.

Drape it in 3D

Place the pieces round a figure built from the measurements and drape them in Blender's cloth simulation. Each material's fabric, from silk to leather, sets how its pieces hang.

Hide, isolate and stack pieces

Pieces drafted over each other stay workable: hide a piece, isolate one to dim everything it doesn't use, or bring it to the front. Every piece still prints.

Lock finished work

Lock a block or piece so it can't be moved, edited or deleted by accident. It can still be selected and built on, and its formulas still follow the measurements.

Five desktop apps, one set of files

Each app does one job. They share plain JSON files, so a pattern made in one opens in the others, and in the Design app in your browser.

opensloper-drape

Command line

opensloper-cli

Check and export patterns, pack layouts, export drapes for Blender, manage measurements, and serve the Automation API to scripts, or to Claude as an MCP server.

File formats are documented, with JSON Schemas generated from the code, so you can read and write patterns from your own tools. See File formats.

Script a pattern, or ask Claude

opensloper-cli serve works through the same steps as the design window, so names, formulas and undo follow the same rules. A script can draft a family of patterns or test one, and Claude can draft, check and fix a pattern, looking at the draft as a picture to see what it made.

  1. Scripts

    JSON-RPC from any language that reads and writes lines of JSON, or opensloper.py, a Python client with a method for each call.

  2. MCP

    serve is also an MCP server, so Claude Code and Claude Desktop can use every method as a tool.

  3. In the window

    Ask Claude from the Claude tab in the desktop design, publish and drape apps, or let Claude Code or any MCP client connect to the open pattern from the Assistance menu. Off until you allow it, and every change is one step you can undo.

  4. Privacy

    Assistants are never told a folder or a measurement file's name, and a preference keeps the measurement values back too. The Claude tab says what is sent before your first question.

Read the scripting guide · Asking Claude

from opensloper import OpenSloper

OpenSloper.create_pattern_file("gathered-skirt.sloper", units="cm",
    measurement_file="measurements/size-table.slm")
with OpenSloper() as sloper:
    sloper.open("gathered-skirt.sloper", size="12")
    sloper.add_variable("fullness", "1.5")
    sloper.add_point("freePoint", name="A", x="0", y="0")
    sloper.add_rectangle("A",
        "@hip_circumference / 2 * #fullness",
        "@waist_to_knee + 4", role="outline")
    sloper.create_piece(50, 50, name="Skirt")
    print(sloper.evaluate()["problems"] or "none")
    sloper.save()

Free and open source, for good

The OpenSloper software costs nothing and always will. It's a community project under the GNU GPL v3, so anyone can use it, study how it works, change it and share it, in the browser as on the desktop.

Free to use

No price and no ads. The desktop apps need no account, and the browser beta no sign-up. Use it for personal sewing, teaching or a business.

Your patterns stay yours

Patterns and measurements are plain, documented JSON files on your own computer, readable by any tool, whichever app you draft them in.

Open code

Every line of the apps, the browser app's drafting engine and editor included, is on GitHub under the GPL, so the software can never be locked away or taken from you.

Built together

Bug reports, ideas, tutorials and code are all welcome. See how to contribute.

Where it's at

OpenSloper {{ version }} is an early release, for trying out: features and file formats may still change. See what's new. The Design, Publish and Measurements apps in the browser are a beta built from the same release; open them in any recent browser.

Download the desktop apps for Linux (x86_64), macOS (Apple silicon) or Windows (x86_64): a .deb for Debian and Ubuntu, a .dmg for macOS and a setup.exe for Windows, or a folder to unpack and run, portable from a USB drive. Each download has the user guide to read offline.

Or build it from source:

git clone --recursive \
  {{ repo }}.git
cd opensloper
./opensloper-make.sh build

You'll need Lazarus 4.8 with Free Pascal 3.2.2, and on Linux the Qt Pascal bindings. The getting started guide has the details.
